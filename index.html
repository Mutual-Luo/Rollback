Rollback

Rewinding World Models to Critical Decisions for Vision-Language-Action Post-Training

Anonymous Authors

Paper soon arXiv soon Code soon Video soon

Abstract

Action-conditioned world models offer a scalable alternative to costly real-world interaction for post-training vision-language-action (VLA) models. However, existing world-model-based methods largely treat world models as forward-only surrogates of the physical environment, leaving untapped a capability no physical environment affords: rewinding to any past state to explore counterfactual futures. In this work, we introduce Rollback, a post-training paradigm that turns world models from passive simulators into counterfactual engines: it rolls back to critical decision points, branches into alternative actions, and contrasts their outcomes to attribute success to individual decisions. This concentrates imagination where it matters most and distills counterfactual outcomes into fine-grained, action-level supervision without a separately learned value model. Furthermore, we build a scalable training system that unifies distributed world-model search with asynchronous real-robot execution, forming a self-reinforcing cycle: real-world experience continually sharpens the world model, and a sharper world model in turn drives further policy improvement. Experiments on three challenging real-world tasks, block extraction, ball tossing, and tabletop hockey, show that Rollback achieves higher success rates than strong baselines and more stable improvement throughout training.

Key Idea

A physical robot cannot undo an action. A world model can. Rollback rewinds imagination to the decisions that determine success, tries alternative actions from the same moment, and learns from how their outcomes differ.
01

Memory-backtracking tree search

Starting from successful and failed real-world trajectories, Rollback restores cached world-model context at intermediate decision points and branches into alternative action chunks. Monte Carlo tree search allocates simulation budget toward decisions whose alternatives lead to divergent outcomes.

02

Tree-structured value estimation

Sibling branches share a decision context, so contrasting their downstream outcomes isolates the effect of individual actions. Subtree-level outcome aggregation yields action-level advantage supervision \(\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{tree}}\) without an additional learned value model.

03

World model–policy co-evolution

A scalable training system couples distributed world-model search with asynchronous real-robot execution. Real trajectories \(\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{real}}\) continually adapt the world model \(\mathcal{W}_\phi\), which in turn supports further improvement of the policy \(\pi_\theta\).

Method

Real-World Tasks

We evaluate on an AgiBot Genie G2 with OmniPicker grippers across three tasks that span contact-rich manipulation, precise control, and competitive interaction.

Block Extraction

Remove blocks one at a time from a stacked structure without making it collapse.

Ball Tossing

Throw a ball into a bucket, which requires precise control of the throwing motion and release timing.

Tabletop Hockey

Play two pucks against a human opponent; later shots can displace earlier pucks, and proximity to the target decides the winner.
